# AI Job Search Kit for Women

## For women who need better work without starting from zero

This kit is for the woman who knows she needs to make a move, but is too tired to rebuild her resume, LinkedIn, applications, and interview prep from scratch.

You do not need to become an AI expert. You need a clear direction, better words for your experience, and a repeatable way to apply without spiraling.

**Best for:** remote job searches, career pivots, women returning to the market, HR/admin/operations/customer support/marketing/general business roles, and anyone who needs practical materials fast.

**Goal for one sitting:**

By the end, you should know:

- what roles you are actually targeting
- what your resume should emphasize
- what your LinkedIn should say
- what to use as your basic outreach message
- how to prep for interviews without freezing
- how to track applications without chaos

---

## How to use this kit in one sitting

Set a timer for 90 minutes.

1. **15 minutes:** choose your target roles.
2. **20 minutes:** audit your resume.
3. **20 minutes:** refresh your LinkedIn headline and About section.
4. **15 minutes:** build your remote job search filters.
5. **10 minutes:** create one outreach message.
6. **10 minutes:** prep three interview stories.

Do not try to make everything perfect. The first win is clarity. You can polish later.

## Before you paste anything into AI

AI gives better answers when you give it context.

Copy and fill this first:

```text
I am job searching and I want practical help, not generic career advice.

My current or most recent role:
My industry:
My strongest tasks/responsibilities:
Tools I have used:
Types of roles I am considering:
Remote / hybrid / onsite preference:
Salary range or minimum:
Things I do not want in my next job:
Tone I want for my materials: clear, confident, human, not fake or overly corporate.
```

Use that context at the top of every prompt in this kit.

## Part 2: Resume audit

Your resume is not your life story. It is a relevance document.

Every bullet should answer: **why should this person trust me for this role?**

### Resume audit checklist

Go through your resume and mark yes/no:

- Is my target role obvious within 10 seconds?
- Does the top section match the jobs I am applying for?
- Do my bullets show outcomes, not just duties?
- Did I include tools, systems, departments, volume, or scope?
- Is anything outdated, distracting, or too personal?
- Are my strongest responsibilities buried too low?
- Do I sound like a real person or like a job description copied itself?

### Prompt: resume positioning audit

```text
Act as a practical resume strategist for women applying to better or remote work.

I am targeting these roles:
[role 1]
[role 2]
[backup role]

Review my resume for positioning, clarity, and relevance.

Tell me:
1. what the resume currently makes me look qualified for
2. what is unclear or too generic
3. which bullets should be rewritten first
4. what keywords I am missing for my target roles
5. what I should remove or reduce
6. a stronger summary section in my voice

Use direct language. Do not make me sound fake, inflated, or overly corporate.

Here is my resume:
[paste resume]
```

### Prompt: rewrite weak bullets

```text
Rewrite these resume bullets so they sound stronger, clearer, and more results-oriented without inventing numbers or lying.

Target role: [target role]
Tone: confident, specific, human, not robotic.

For each bullet:
- keep it truthful
- make the work sound more valuable
- add scope if the original includes it
- suggest where I could add a real number if I know it

Current bullets:
[paste bullets]
```

### Bullet formula

Use this structure when you get stuck:

**Did what + for whom/what + using what + result or purpose**

Examples:

- Managed weekly onboarding tasks for new hires across multiple store locations, keeping documents, reminders, and manager follow-up organized.
- Coordinated scheduling and employee communication between leadership and store teams to reduce missed updates and last-minute confusion.
- Maintained HR files, compliance documents, and internal records with a focus on accuracy, privacy, and fast retrieval.

## Part 3: LinkedIn refresh

LinkedIn should make your target obvious. It does not need to sound like a TED Talk.

### LinkedIn headline options

Use this prompt:

```text
Write 10 LinkedIn headline options for me.

Target roles:
[role 1]
[role 2]

My background:
[paste short context]

Rules:
- no buzzwords like guru, ninja, visionary, or passionate professional
- make me sound credible and human
- include keywords recruiters might search
- give me a mix of straightforward, warm, and slightly more polished options
```

Pick one that feels clear, not clever.

### LinkedIn About section prompt

```text
Write a LinkedIn About section for me based on the context below.

I want it to sound like a real professional woman, not a corporate template.

Structure:
1. first line: what I help with or what kind of work I do
2. short paragraph about my experience
3. short paragraph about what I am looking for next
4. short list of strengths/tools/areas
5. simple closing line inviting relevant opportunities

Avoid inflated claims. Keep it clear, warm, and practical.

Context:
[paste background, target roles, strengths, tools]
```

### Featured section idea

If you do not have a portfolio, use your Featured section for:

- a short resume PDF
- a simple one-page work sample
- a post about the type of work you are seeking
- a certification or project if relevant

Do not overload it. One to three items is enough.

## Part 4: Remote job search filters

Remote job searching gets messy fast. Your filters matter.

### Good search phrases

Try combinations like:

- remote HR coordinator
- remote HR assistant
- remote people operations coordinator
- remote recruiting coordinator
- remote operations coordinator
- remote administrative coordinator
- remote customer operations
- remote client success coordinator
- remote executive assistant operations
- remote onboarding coordinator
- remote employee experience coordinator
- remote compliance coordinator
- remote training coordinator

Swap in your industry or tools:

- healthcare
- retail
- hospitality
- SaaS
- nonprofit
- payroll
- onboarding
- scheduling
- compliance
- employee relations

### Prompt: build my job search query list

```text
Create a job search query list for me.

My target roles are:
[roles]

My must-haves:
[remote/hybrid, salary minimum, schedule, industry preferences]

My background:
[paste context]

Give me:
1. 20 exact job search phrases to use on LinkedIn, Indeed, Google, and company sites
2. 10 role titles I may not know to search for
3. red flags to avoid
4. keywords to add to my resume and LinkedIn
5. a simple weekly search plan
```

### Remote job red flags

Be careful with:

- “commission only” unless you truly want sales
- vague assistant roles with no company info
- jobs asking for payment, equipment purchase, or crypto setup
- remote listings with no salary range and huge responsibilities
- “must be available 24/7” language
- companies with no real website or employee footprint

## Part 5: Outreach without sounding desperate

You do not need a dramatic networking message. You need a short, normal note.

### Recruiter message

```text
Hi [Name], I saw the [role title] opening and wanted to reach out directly. My background is in [your lane], especially [strength 1], [strength 2], and [strength 3]. I’m looking for a remote or hybrid role where I can support [team/function/outcome]. I applied and would love to be considered if the role is still active. Thank you.
```

### Warm connection message

```text
Hi [Name], I’m exploring remote roles in [target area] and thought of you because of your work in [company/industry]. If you see anything aligned with [role type], I’d be grateful if you kept me in mind. No pressure at all. I’m mainly trying to be more intentional with my search.
```

### Prompt: customize my outreach

```text
Write 5 short LinkedIn outreach messages for me.

Context:
- I am applying for: [role]
- Company: [company]
- Why I am interested: [reason]
- My relevant experience: [experience]

Rules:
- under 90 words
- human and calm
- not needy
- not fake excited
- no em dashes
- include one version for a recruiter, one for a hiring manager, one for a warm connection, one after applying, and one follow-up
```

## Part 6: Interview prep

Interviews get easier when you have stories ready.

Prepare three stories:

1. A time you organized something messy.
2. A time you handled a difficult person or situation.
3. A time you learned a tool, process, or responsibility quickly.

### Story worksheet

**Situation:** what was happening?

**Task:** what were you responsible for?

**Action:** what did you do?

**Result:** what changed, improved, got completed, or became clearer?

### Prompt: build my interview stories

```text
Help me turn these work experiences into interview stories using the STAR format.

Target role: [role]
Tone: clear, professional, not rehearsed.

For each story:
1. write a 60-second version
2. write a shorter 30-second version
3. list the interview questions this story could answer
4. suggest one detail I should add if I know it

Experiences:
[paste 3 situations]
```

### Questions to practice

- Tell me about yourself.
- Why are you interested in this role?
- Why are you leaving your current role?
- Tell me about a time you handled a difficult situation.
- How do you stay organized while working remotely?
- What tools or systems have you used?
- What type of manager helps you do your best work?
- What are your salary expectations?

### Salary language

```text
Based on the role scope and my experience, I’m targeting a range around [range], but I’m open to discussing the full package and expectations for the position.
```

Do not give your lowest number first unless you have to.

## Part 7: Application tracker

Use a simple tracker. Do not create a second job called “managing the spreadsheet.”

Columns:

- Company
- Role
- Link
- Salary/range
- Remote/hybrid/onsite
- Date applied
- Resume version used
- Contact person
- Follow-up date
- Status
- Notes/red flags

### Weekly rhythm

**Monday:** search and save roles.

**Tuesday/Wednesday:** apply to best-fit roles.

**Thursday:** outreach/follow-ups.

**Friday:** update tracker and improve one resume/LinkedIn section.

Aim for quality, not panic-applying to 100 jobs that do not fit.

## Final note

You are not behind. You are overwhelmed because the process has too many moving pieces.

Use AI to reduce the blank page. Keep your judgment. Keep your voice. Apply with a plan.
